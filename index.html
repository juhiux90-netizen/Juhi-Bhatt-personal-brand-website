The Thread

Many rooms. One way of working.

Psychology, classrooms, inclusion, mentorship, program leadership — from the outside, different jobs. From the inside, the same craft, aimed at a wider circle of people each time.

01

Who I am

I'm someone who has spent more than a decade close to people — as a psychologist, an educator, a mentor to teams, and now the person holding together an entire hub of people, programs and stakeholders. My work has always started with the same question: what does this person, or this system, actually need — and how do we get there?

02

What connects my work

School counselling. Special education. Program leadership. UX research. And now, leading a hub of people, projects and partners at Podar. On paper, these look like different jobs. In practice, they're the same craft — observing behaviour, listening closely, and shaping the structure around a person so the right outcome becomes possible.

03

How I think

I think in systems and in individuals, at the same time. I've built plans for a single child and led frameworks that run across teams and stakeholders. I trust evidence, iterate on feedback, and turn messy, human situations into structures people can actually work within.

04

Where this has led

That throughline is now my job description. As Hub Incharge at Podar, I hold together people, projects, learning and stakeholders — using the instinct I've always had: understand the person, then build the system around them that lets them succeed.

Psychology, Applied

People are never just data points.

My training is in psychology, but my work has never lived only in a counselling room. It shows up in how a program is designed, how a difficult stakeholder conversation is handled, and how a team is built so that people don't burn out.

What I bring to every room isn't a diagnosis — it's attention. To behaviour, to context, to the small signals that explain why something is or isn't working, for a person or for a whole system.

  • Behaviour Reading what's underneath a reaction, not just the reaction itself.
  • Reflection Slowing down enough to ask what's actually needed, not just what's asked for.
  • Connection Building trust with students, teachers, parents and teams alike.
  • Growth Designing conditions where people can improve, not just be evaluated.

Philosophy

People aren't problems. Systems are.

01

Nobody I've ever worked with was a problem to be solved. A child struggling in class, a teacher burning out, a team missing its targets — in every case, the story wasn't about a person failing. It was about a system that hadn't been built for them yet.

02

Behaviour rarely lies. It usually points to the structure around it — the process, the workload, the way support is, or isn't, offered. Change the system, and the behaviour it was quietly reacting to tends to change too.

03

People learn, contribute and grow when they feel genuinely supported — not managed, not fixed, just met where they are. That's as true in a counselling room as it is in a stakeholder meeting.

04

So the work, wherever I've done it, has stayed the same: understand the person in front of me, and understand the system around them — because real, lasting change needs both.
